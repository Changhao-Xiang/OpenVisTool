This work addresses a fundamental scientific question: \emph{what makes a visual tool-use trajectory learnable?} Our answer is that a trajectory is suitable for supervision only when it simultaneously satisfies \textbf{outcome correctness}—the final answer is verifiably right—and \textbf{causal observation utility}—each intermediate tool call provably contributes to reaching that answer. This insight directly guided the construction of \textsc{OpenVisTool}, where a dual-filter pipeline retains only trajectories that are both correct and explanatorily minimal, yielding a compact yet high-signal training corpus that generalizes across five visually grounded domains. More broadly, we believe the \emph{outcome $\times$ causality} framing provides a principled and transferable framework for future agent supervision: as tool-augmented models grow in capability, curating demonstrations that are not merely successful but structurally grounded will be essential for scalable imitation beyond end-reward filtering alone.
