Models actively transform, inspect, and verify visual evidence during the reasoning process \citep{su2025thinkingsurvey}. Recent works instantiate this paradigm through a tool-calling agent interface, in which image operations are actions and their outputs become intermediate evidence. They mainly differ in how the tool-use capability is obtained: training-free prompting \citep{hu2024sketchpad,zhao2025pyvision}, reinforcement learning over visual tool use \citep{zheng2026deepeyes,wu2026vtoolr}, and cold-start SFT followed by RL for robust multi-step tool use \citep{su2025openthinkimg,su2026pixelreasoner,hong2026deepeyesv2,zhang2026thyme,lai2026minio3,guo2025codevision,hou2025codev,zhao2026pyvisionrl}. Rather than proposing another training algorithm, \method is an open, cross-domain recipe for synthesizing and filtering visual tool-use trajectories.

\paragraph{Visual Tool-Use Learning}
Recent work on teaching multimodal models to use visual tools follows two paradigms. One directly optimizes tool-calling policies via reinforcement learning \citep{zheng2026deepeyes, wu2026vtoolr}, but RL alone often fails to bootstrap stable tool invocation without a well-initialized policy. The dominant recipe therefore adopts a two-stage approach: cold-start supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on teacher-generated trajectories to establish basic tool-use competence, followed by RL for further refinement \citep{su2025openthinkimg, su2026pixelreasoner, hong2026deepeyesv2, lai2026minio3, guo2025codevision, hou2025codev}. Under this paradigm, SFT quality directly determines whether RL can converge to capable policies \citep{hong2026deepeyesv2, zhang2026thyme}. Yet existing work largely treats the SFT corpus as a commodity—any answer-correct teacher trajectory is assumed to provide useful supervision. What constitutes genuinely beneficial supervision for visual tool-use learning has not been systematically studied.

\paragraph{Supervision Quality for Reasoning}
In text-based reasoning, the quality of training data has received sustained attention. Rejection sampling—retaining only solutions that reach the correct final answer—is a standard recipe for curating reasoning corpora \citep{zelikman2022star, yuan2023scaling}. Subsequent studies demonstrate that not all correct traces are equally instructive: process-level verification reveals that individual reasoning steps vary in correctness and informativeness \citep{lightman2024let, wang2024math}, and difficulty-aware or diversity-aware selection further improves learning efficiency over naive outcome filtering \citep{yu2024metamath}. These findings establish a clear lesson: supervision quality matters as much as quantity for learning to reason. However, this lesson has yet to be transferred to the visual tool-use setting, where the notion of ``quality'' is further complicated by tool observations—a returned modality whose actual contribution to reasoning is neither guaranteed nor straightforward to assess.

\paragraph{Visual Evidence Acquisition}
The paradigm of ``thinking with images,'' introduced by OpenAI's o3 \citep{openai2025o3}, reframes visual reasoning as an active evidence-acquisition process: rather than relying on a single static encoding, models iteratively crop, zoom, or render visual inputs and incorporate the resulting observations into their reasoning \citep{su2025thinkingsurvey}. Under this lens, visual tools are instruments for obtaining evidence that the model's intrinsic visual encoding cannot provide. This perspective motivates our causal framing of supervision selection: if the purpose of a tool call is to acquire otherwise-unavailable evidence, then a trajectory is instructive only when its tool observations causally contribute to reaching the correct answer. Trajectories where tools are invoked but the answer could have been obtained without the returned observations teach \emph{correlation}—tool calls co-occur with success—rather than the deeper skill of knowing \emph{when and why} to seek external visual evidence.

In text-only tool-integrated reasoning, prior methods synthesize traces for coding, search, or general tool-calling agents \citep{schick2023toolformer,qin2024toolllm,liu2024apigen,chen2023fireact,zeng2024agenttuning}. Multimodal agentic systems extend this idea to visual evidence, distilling trajectories that interleave reasoning, image operations, and returned observations from strong teachers \citep{gao2025multimodal,zhang2026thyme,lai2026minio3,cheng2026openmobilebuildingopenmobile}. In recent trajectory-distillation pipelines, a common recipe is outcome-based rejection sampling: candidate trajectories are generated, and those that are well-formed, executable, and answer-correct are retained. Such filtering ensures that a demonstration reaches the right answer, but it does not establish that the recorded observations were useful, since successful teacher traces may still contain incidental calls. In contrast, \method retains only trajectories that are both answer-correct and tool-beneficial, so the supervision is selected for evidence acquisition rather than tool-call mimicry.
