\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Large language models become markedly more capable when they can call external tools during reasoning instead of relying on a single forward pass. A code interpreter offloads the precise arithmetic and symbolic manipulation that free-form generation tends to get wrong \citep{gao2023pal,feng2025retool}, while search and retrieval inject knowledge that lies outside the model's parameters \citep{yao2023react,schick2023toolformer,jin2025searchr1}. The common mechanism is that a tool call returns an observation the model could not have produced on its own, and the model conditions its subsequent reasoning on that observation. Multimodal models face an analogous limit, except that the missing information is visual: a single static encoding of an image often fails to expose what a question actually requires.

Visual tools relieve this bottleneck in two complementary ways. First, they \emph{surface evidence} that a fixed view cannot resolve---cropping or zooming to read small axis labels in a chart, aligning a row and column across a low-contrast table, or locating a small or occluded object in a cluttered scene. Second, they \emph{close a generate--verify loop}, turning the model's own output back into a visual observation---for instance, rendering generated HTML and comparing the resulting screenshot against a reference layout. In both regimes the model should act on the visual input---inspect, transform, mark, or render---and feed the result back into its reasoning chain rather than answer from a single static view: the paradigm of \emph{thinking with images} \citep{openai2025o3,su2025thinkingsurvey}.

These capabilities are instilled by training, and the prevailing recipe for agentic vision models is a two-stage pipeline: a cold-start supervised fine-tuning (SFT) stage on curated visual tool-use trajectories, followed by reinforcement learning (RL) that refines the policy. Recent systems such as DeepEyesV2 \citep{hong2026deepeyesv2}, Thyme \citep{zhang2026theme}, and Mini-o3 \citep{lai2026minio3} all follow this template, and several report that RL alone---without an SFT cold start on curated trajectories---fails to bootstrap stable tool use, making the cold-start stage indispensable rather than an optional warm-up. This places the cold-start trajectory corpus at the center of the pipeline. Yet such corpora are typically assembled in-house, specialized to one or two domains such as high-resolution visual search, and seldom released, leaving no general, reusable source of visual tool-use supervision. We therefore treat the data---not the training algorithm---as the lever \method pulls.

Building such a corpus well is harder than it appears, because a useful trajectory must record the interaction \emph{process}---what the model chose to inspect or produce, what the tool returned, and how that observation changed its subsequent reasoning---not merely the final answer. The construction recipe shared across the in-house pipelines above is to distill from a strong teacher and keep a trajectory whenever its final answer is correct. This filter is too weak: a teacher can reach the right answer through unnecessary tool calls, so final-answer correctness reveals neither whether the task needed tools at all nor whether the recorded observations actually helped. A corpus assembled this way teaches the model to imitate tool-use syntax rather than to invoke tools when they genuinely help.

We therefore argue that a retained trajectory must satisfy two conditions: the task should benefit from tools, and the recorded tool outputs should measurably improve solving it. We operationalize both with \method, an open recipe that curates trajectories by double rejection sampling. We first discard queries a base model already solves without tools, so that external evidence has room to help. We then roll out a strong teacher under a unified visual toolset and keep a trajectory only when it is both \emph{answer-correct}, verified by domain-appropriate evaluators (rule-based matching, an LLM judge, or render-based VLM judging for Web-to-HTML), and \emph{tool-gainful}: prepending the teacher's tool observations to a base model's context must improve its accuracy on the query. The main \dataset is the intersection of the two sets.

The resulting \dataset contains 42K trajectories spanning five domains chosen for complementary visual bottlenecks: Chart and Table stress fine-grained reading and structural alignment, GUI Grounding stresses local inspection and coordinate precision, Visual Search stresses active evidence search in cluttered scenes, and Web-to-HTML stresses a generate-render-observe-revise loop. All domains share one visual toolset---cropping, marking, color and structure analysis, rendering, and final action expression---so the model learns reusable operations rather than a separate interface per task.

Fine-tuning on \dataset improves all five domains across four backbones spanning two model families and 4B--27B scale. On Qwen3.5-9B the average rises from 32.6 to 45.8, with the largest gains where tools expose otherwise-hidden evidence (Visual Search +23.8, Table +17.3). Controlled variants confirm the design: correctness-only and tool-gain-only filtering each underperform their intersection, and against a no-tool trajectory SFT baseline trained on the same queries, tool-use supervision improves the average by 8.7 points. We devote a dedicated analysis to disentangling the contributions of double rejection sampling and tool-use supervision, and to examining where tool use helps most (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}). Overall, the advantage of visual tool-use distillation is sharpest when tools acquire new visual evidence.

In summary, our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item We present \method, an open recipe for synthesizing cross-domain visual tool-use trajectories. Its core is \emph{double rejection sampling}: we discard queries a base model already solves without tools, and retain a trajectory only when it is both answer-correct and \toolgain---its recorded observations measurably raise a base model's accuracy---so the supervision teaches evidence acquisition rather than tool-call mimicry.
  \item We release \dataset, a 42K-trajectory corpus spanning Chart, GUI Grounding, Table, Visual Search, and Web-to-HTML under one shared visual toolset, with domains chosen for complementary visual bottlenecks, offered as open cold-start supervision for agentic vision models.
  \item We show that fine-tuning on \dataset improves all five domains across four backbones spanning two model families and 4B--27B scale, and provide controlled variants and a dedicated analysis establishing that the correctness and \toolgain filters are complementary and that tool-use supervision outperforms no-tool trajectories on evidence-acquisition domains.
\end{itemize}
